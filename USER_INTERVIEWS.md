# User Interviews

Notes from three real conversations with potential users (10-15 minutes each). Conducted during the build week to validate the AI Spend Audit concept and sharpen product decisions.

---

## Interview 1 -- Priya

**Role:** Assistant Professor  
**Organization:** ABES Engineering College  
**Company stage:** Academic institution (individual educator)  
**Conducted:** 2026-05-27, 12 minutes

I spoke with Priya, an Assistant Professor at ABES Engineering College, about how she uses AI tools in her daily academic workflow. She currently pays for Claude Pro (~$20/month) and also uses the free versions of ChatGPT and Cursor. Her primary use cases are question generation, preparing academic material, and solving doubts for students.

She mentioned that Claude gives better quality outputs for educational tasks compared to other tools, but also said that managing multiple AI platforms is expensive and inefficient. She was interested in the idea of a unified platform where users could access multiple AI models from one place without paying for many subscriptions individually.

### Direct Quotes

- "I use Claude for question generation and solving student doubts."
- "Using different models is expensive and inefficient."
- "I need one place where I can access multiple models, but it should also be fast."

### Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising thing she said was that the tool would be more useful for companies or organizations than for individuals. I originally assumed individual users would also strongly care about AI spend optimization, but she felt the savings for individuals are usually too small to matter.

### What It Changed About My Design

This conversation changed my understanding of the target audience. I realized the product should focus more on organizations and teams rather than solo users. It also made me think about adding comparisons between multiple AI models and showing consolidated alternatives for overlapping subscriptions.

## Interview 2 -- Dinesh

**Role:** Shop Owner  
**Business:** Dry Cleaning Business  
**Company stage:** Small enterprise (4 staff members)  
**Conducted:** 2026-05-27, 14 minutes

I spoke with Dinesh, who runs a dry-cleaning business with four staff members. Surprisingly, his business already relies heavily on AI tools in day-to-day operations. They currently use four Claude subscriptions, mainly for customer discovery, promotional template generation, and WhatsApp marketing campaigns.

Three staff members use Claude to analyze existing customer information and identify potential customers from their database, while one staff member uses it to generate templates and promotional content for online campaigns. Dinesh said the biggest challenge is the overwhelming number of AI tools entering the market every day, making it difficult to identify which tools are genuinely useful and provide the best value for money.

### Direct Quotes

- "New AI tools are launching every day, it's hard to know which one is actually useful and mostly value for money."
- "Three staff members use Claude to find potential customers from our existing customer data."
- "I'd definitely use software that compares AI spending and tells me where I'm overspending."

### Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising part was discovering that even a relatively small local business was already paying for multiple AI subscriptions and using them operationally. I did not expect a dry-cleaning business to actively depend on AI for customer targeting and marketing.

### What It Changed About My Design

This interview made me realize that if a small business with only four employees already has multiple paid AI subscriptions, then slightly larger businesses could easily have 10-20 subscriptions across teams. It validated the idea that AI spend optimization is a real and growing problem for businesses, especially small and medium-sized organizations.

## Interview 3 -- Harish

**Role:** Software Development Engineer 2  
**Company:** Amazon  
**Company stage:** Large enterprise (individual developer)  
**Conducted:** 2026-05-27, 13 minutes

I spoke with Harish, an SDE-2 at Amazon, about his AI coding workflow and spending habits. He currently pays for Claude, Codex, and Cursor subscriptions, spending roughly $80-100 per month on AI tools. Interestingly, he admitted that he still pays for Cursor even though he barely uses it anymore.

Harish strongly preferred Codex as a coding assistant and believed it currently provides the best value among coding-focused AI tools. He also mentioned that developers often accumulate multiple subscriptions because different tools excel at different tasks, but over time many subscriptions become underused. He was highly interested in a dashboard that could continuously monitor AI subscriptions and identify which ones are not providing enough value.

### Direct Quotes

- "Codex is currently the best coding helper I've used."
- "I still pay for Cursor even though I barely use it."
- "Developers need a dashboard that shows which AI subscriptions are actually worth it."

### Most Surprising Insight

The most surprising insight was that even experienced developers working at large companies continue paying for subscriptions they rarely use simply because switching tools or cancelling subscriptions feels inconvenient.

### What It Changed About My Design

This interview changed my thinking from a one-time audit product into a more continuous monitoring system. I realized many employed developers and teams keep unused or underused subscriptions active, which creates an opportunity for ongoing spend tracking and subscription optimization dashboards instead of static reports.
